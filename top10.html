The Owner's Top 10

The ten things every owner should be doing with AI right now. Each expands into: why it matters, the exact steps, the agents, the KPI, a 30-day quick win, and the mistake that sinks most people.

1 Diagnose before you deploy ▸

Why

Tools bought before diagnosis become subscriptions you forget. Twenty minutes of measurement makes every later decision obvious — you deploy against your actual weakest point, not the shiniest demo.

Exact steps

  1. Run the Business DNA scan (12 questions) — get your code and gap prescriptions.
  2. Run the Time Gap Analyzer with honest hours.
  3. Email yourself both spreadsheets — they're your baseline.
  4. Circle your weakest strand + your top 3 time leaks. That's your 30-day battlefield.

KPI

Reclaimable hours identified; DNA vitality score (re-scan quarterly).

30-day quick win

Most owners find 8+ reclaimable hours in the first audit — before deploying anything.

The mistake

Skipping straight to tools. A tool without a target is a toy with a monthly fee.

2 Hire named agents, not casual prompts ▸

Why

A prompt is a one-off request; a role is a relationship. Agents with names, role cards, standards and boundaries produce senior-level output because your management is senior-level.

Exact steps

  1. Pick your first hire from the AI Family — Slate (inbox) is the classic first win.
  2. Copy the hire prompt; add 3 lines of your context (what you sell, to whom, your voice).
  3. Test on already-solved work; grade against your own versions.
  4. Paste in 2–3 examples of excellent work — taste transfers via examples.
  5. Go live with a daily review gate. Hire agent #2 only after #1 saves you an hour a week.

KPI

# of active agents with role cards; hours saved per agent per week.

30-day quick win

Inbox agent alone typically returns 3–5 hrs/week within a fortnight.

The mistake

Hiring ten agents in a weekend. Each needs a week of onboarding attention — stagger them.

3 Install a weekly content engine ▸

Why

Consistency beats brilliance in every algorithm and every memory. An engine converts 15 minutes of your raw truth into a week of presence — without you becoming a content creator.

Exact steps

  1. Monday: record 15 minutes of voice notes — one customer story, one opinion, answers to 3 buyer questions.
  2. Quill drafts the pillar piece from your transcript, stories intact.
  3. Remix splits it into 15 platform-native assets.
  4. Echo passes everything through your voice guide.
  5. Cadence schedules to your true capacity. Full build in Module 6.

KPI

Publishing consistency (weeks hit ÷ weeks); assets shipped per hour of your time.

30-day quick win

Four straight weeks of consistent output — often the first time ever.

The mistake

Letting AI invent generic content. The engine's fuel is YOUR stories; without them it's beige noise.

4 Claim your shelf in AI search (AEO) ▸

Why

Buyers increasingly ask AI assistants, and assistants answer with citations. Whoever becomes the cited answer in a category owns pre-sold trust at near-zero acquisition cost. Almost no small business is competing for this yet.

Exact steps

  1. Ask 3 AI assistants your category questions ("best ___ in ___") — record who gets cited.
  2. Run Lumen's AEO audit on your top 3 pages: extractable answers, entity clarity, FAQs.
  3. Publish definitive answer content: real data, named expertise, clear structure.
  4. For local businesses: pair with Maple's local-presence checklist.
  5. Re-test monthly; log citations.

KPI

AI citations per month across assistants; % of category questions where you appear.

30-day quick win

One definitive FAQ page often earns first citations within weeks in low-competition niches.

The mistake

Keyword-stuffing like 2015 SEO. Answer engines reward clarity and evidence, not repetition.

5 Automate every follow-up ▸

Why

The most expensive silence in business is the follow-up that never happened: quotes unanswered, carts abandoned, happy customers never asked for reviews. Follow-up is pure system — which means agents can run all of it.

Exact steps

  1. Map every follow-up moment: quote sent, job done, cart left, invoice due, customer gone quiet.
  2. Compass designs pipeline cadences; Cartwright handles carts; Ripple triggers review asks; Toll chases invoices; Encore runs win-backs.
  3. Write each message once, in your voice (Echo), with a clear trigger.
  4. Review gate for the first month, then let it run with weekly spot checks.

KPI

Follow-ups sent per week (was ~0); revenue recovered from follow-up sequences.

30-day quick win

Quote follow-up alone typically lifts close rate 10–25% — same leads, more yeses.

The mistake

Making automated messages sound automated. Every sequence gets your voice pass first.

6 Run the 13-week cash forecast ▸

Why

Cash surprises kill more small businesses than competition does. A rolling 13-week forecast turns crunches into calendar items you solve two months early — with a phone call instead of a loan.

Exact steps

  1. Friday ritual: give Stream your expected inflows/outflows.
  2. Get the 13-week table; mark the tightest weeks.
  3. For each tight week, pick a lever early: invoice sooner, shift a payment, pre-sell.
  4. Almanac layers in your seasonality for the 12-month view.

KPI

Surprise-free weeks streak; days of cash runway at the lowest point.

30-day quick win

The first forecast usually reveals one collectible invoice or delayable expense worth 4 figures.

The mistake

Building it once and never updating. The power is the Friday rhythm, not the spreadsheet.

7 Run a quarterly profit sprint ▸

Why

There is almost always 8–20% profit hiding in expenses, receivables, pricing and margin mix. These analyses used to cost more than they returned; now they cost an afternoon. See the Profit Lab.

Exact steps

  1. Days 1–2: Hawk's expense autopsy + Toll's receivables chase.
  2. Days 3–4: Margin ranks every offer by profit-per-hour including your time.
  3. Day 5: Meter's pricing review — pick ONE increase to test on new customers.
  4. Days 6–7: Stream's forecast + write the one-page findings memo.

KPI

Dollars found per sprint; profit margin trend quarter over quarter.

30-day quick win

Zombie subscriptions and uncollected invoices — most owners find four figures in week one.

The mistake

Raising prices on everyone at once. Test on new customers first; grandfather the loyal.

8 Read the market weekly (intelligence swarm) ▸

Why

Big companies have analyst departments; you have a swarm. Knowing what competitors changed, what's trending and where you stand — every Monday — means you move first, price smarter, and never get blindsided.

Exact steps

  1. Configure the five: Vantage (competitors), Pulse (trends), Halo (reviews — yours and rivals'), Drift (benchmarks), Lens (synthesis).
  2. Monday: run the swarm; Lens merges everything into a one-page brief.
  3. Each brief ends with "one action this week" — do it.
  4. Keep a running log; patterns across weeks are the real gold.

KPI

Briefings read streak; actions taken from briefings; days-to-respond to competitor moves.

30-day quick win

The first competitor scan almost always finds an exploitable gap — a dropped service, a pricing hole, an unanswered complaint pattern.

The mistake

Collecting intelligence you never act on. The brief's last line is an order, not trivia.

9 Adopt a 1-page AI policy + review gates ▸

Why

One hallucinated fact shipped to a customer can cancel a year of AI goodwill. A one-page policy — what's approved, what's forbidden, what needs human eyes — protects your brand and reassures your team that AI is their assistant, not their replacement.

Exact steps

  1. Ethica drafts your policy: approved uses, prohibited uses, disclosure language, data that never enters prompts, review requirements.
  2. Rule of thumb: anything customer-facing, financial or irreversible gets human review.
  3. Show the team; assign each person one agent as THEIR assistant.
  4. Log incidents (target: zero) and near-misses; update the policy quarterly.

KPI

Incidents shipped (target 0); % of team actively using approved AI.

30-day quick win

Team resistance usually flips to enthusiasm the week each person gets an agent that kills their most-hated task.

The mistake

Secrecy. Teams that discover the agents by accident assume layoffs and resist everything.

10 Keep one experiment always running ▸

Why

The frontier moves monthly (watch the Radar). Owners who maintain one small, bounded experiment compound learning while rivals wait for certainty that never comes.

Exact steps

  1. Pick one Radar signal marked "Act now" you haven't touched.
  2. Probe designs the cheapest 2-week test with kill/go criteria written BEFORE you start.
  3. Cap the budget (time and money) at what you'd happily lose.
  4. Conclude every experiment with a verdict: adopt, park, or kill — then start the next.

KPI

Experiments concluded per quarter; adoption rate of winners.

30-day quick win

Two concluded experiments — even two "kills" — put you ahead of the 90% of owners still reading headlines.

The mistake

Experiments without kill criteria. They become zombie projects that eat the time AI just saved you.
